Gadgets for majority dynamics on trees

Step through the pieces of a reduction from monotone circuits to synchronous majority on trees whose vertices have degree 1, 3 or 5. Every frame below is an exact state of the dynamics. The circuit compiler turns any Boolean circuit into the macrocells, the program and the clocks of the reduction, and runs it.

Result: predicting synchronous majority is P-complete on trees of maximum degree 5. The proof is analytic and comes from the project's research notes (October 2026), with internal review; this site re-implements the construction and checks it by direct simulation. It is not a peer-reviewed publication.

The rule and the code

The rule

Every vertex is in state 0 or 1 and all vertices update at once. A vertex takes the majority state of its neighbours and keeps its own state on a tie. All degrees here are 1, 3 or 5, so there are no ties: a leaf simply copies its neighbour one step later.

Clocks are trees

A word of bits is played by a private subtree, glued to its consumer by one edge. Three of the four branches of every node always vote for the next bit, so the port follows its word whatever the consumer does. Programs of such clocks move and combine bits stored in macrocells.

Reading the figures

All vertices obey the same rule; colors only distinguish roles. Ports are outlined, and vertices whose history is forced (the consumer, or the abstract controls of the compiler) are dashed.

    Circuit compiler

    Describe a Boolean circuit by its adjacency matrix, or as a list of gates. The compiler builds the tree of the degree-5 reduction: one macrocell per register of a bus, a serial program that reads, combines and writes every gate, and the clocks that play the program.

    Paste a 0/1 matrix

    One row per line, entries separated by spaces or commas (JSON also works). If the size changes, columns without incoming edges become inputs and the others gates; then choose each gate type.

    Syntax: inputs: a=1, b=0, c, then one gate per line such as g = AND(a, b) or m = OR(g, c, a), and finally output: m. Types: AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR. Gates with more than two inputs are split into binary gates.

    Each register of the bus is a macrocell A–C–B with two leaves; each of A, C, B has one external port and two controls.     Full tree

    Every vertex of the tree, without the macrocell summary: macrocells, leaves, constants and all the clocks. Start at any vertex of a macrocell. A box is the whole subtree behind one edge, with its number of vertices; its color is the state of its first vertex. Select a box to unfold it one level, and an unfolded vertex to fold it again. Unfolded clock vertices are simulated with the majority rule. A folded subtree only touches the rest through one edge, so it is replaced exactly by the proven history of its first vertex.

    How the compiler works

    The circuit is first rewritten with AND and OR gates only: negations are pushed to the inputs, and XOR and XNOR are expanded. The inputs, with their values, become constant nodes 1, …, and the gates follow in order, each computed from two earlier nodes. With M nodes in all, the tree has 3M + 5 macrocells:

    • a bus g – b1 – … – bM, and for every node i an arm bi – ci – ri: the register ri keeps the value of node i behind its connector ci;
    • two working arms g – cA – rA and g – cB – rB for the operands.

    A macrocell is a path A – C – B with a leaf on each end. Each of its three main vertices has one external port, two controls and two memory neighbours: degree 5. With u of its controls in 1, a main vertex computes the AND (u = 0), the majority (u = 1) or the OR (u = 2) of its other three neighbours. Hence:

    • HOLD (u = 1 everywhere): a stored bit is kept whatever the ports do;
    • RESET (u = 0 for 4 steps): the macrocell is cleared, given one port that stays 0;
    • LOAD (u = 2 for 4 steps): from 0, it loads the OR of its ports; COPY is RESET then LOAD (8 steps).

    For every gate the program reads its two operands through the bus without erasing them, computes the AND or the OR in macrocell g in 27 steps, writes the result into its register and cleans up: 12(p + q + i) + 147 steps for gate i with operands p and q. The program is played by the controls: each control has a word, and a private degree-5 generator plays that word, robustly, after a common delay S. With H = 200M² + 200, S = ⌈log₂(H + 2)⌉ + 8 and T = S + H, the output register holds the value of the circuit from the end of the program on; it is read at T. The optional EVER receiver z becomes 1 at T + 2 if and only if the output is 1, and is 0 before.

    The page simulates the macrocells exactly, with every control following its word. The generators that produce those words are shown in the Gadgets tab, and they were checked separately against arbitrary consumers; complete trees with real generators were also simulated for small circuits (see Reproducibility).

    How the pieces fit

    Clocks

    Constants are cherries; a timer is two identical arms that OR a wave towards the port; a pulse is a chain in which three of four children always agree with the next bit. A word with K changes is split among four branches by a rotating dissident, so that each branch inherits about half of the changes, until only pulses, timers and constants remain. Every clock has degree at most 5 and is robust to its consumer.

    Memory and program

    Macrocells keep, clear, copy and combine bits according to how many of their controls are 1. A bus with registers on arms of length two evaluates any monotone circuit gate by gate, re-reading registers to serve any fan-out. The whole tree has polynomial size and the answer is ready at a polynomial time.

    Reproducibility

    The page computes everything from assets/tree-model.js. It follows the construction of the research notes on maximum degree 5, which were re-implemented independently in Python and checked by a direct simulation of the majority rule:

    • pulses, generators of every word with up to 10 bits and long sampled words, against adversarial consumers;
    • the macrocell modes against arbitrary ports, and the whole program for every circuit with up to four nodes and random circuits with up to twelve, with every input assignment and the EVER receiver;
    • complete trees with real generators for small circuits, compared vertex by vertex with the macrocell level.

    The site's own test compares this page's model with that Python implementation:

    node tools/test_model.cjs                # the page's model against the Python reference
python3 -B tools/expand_tree.py plan.json # the full tree of a downloaded plan, as an edge list
python3 -B tools/build_standalone.py      # single-file version in dist/
